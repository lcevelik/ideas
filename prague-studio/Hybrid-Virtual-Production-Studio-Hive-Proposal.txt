Hybrid Virtual Production Studio — Concept Plan
A Proposal for Two Stages Within Studio's Hive Program
Executive Summary
The Hive Program
Studio's Hive program is bringing rental houses, production 
companies, post-production, and sound design together 
under one roof across 11 stages.
This proposal carves out 2 of those 11 stages as a dedicated 
hybrid virtual production offering — combining a traditional 
LED volume (ICVFX) stage with a next-generation AI-driven 
grey-box stage, designed to work independently or, ideally, 
together as complementary tools on the same production.
The goal isn't to compete with the other 9 traditional stages, 
but to give Hive tenants and outside productions a reason to 
shoot inside the ecosystem rather than renting LED or VFX 
services elsewhere.
The Core Idea
Most virtual production conversations force a binary choice: build a real-time LED volume, or shoot green/grey screen and composite later. This proposal treats that as a false choice.
STAGE A
LED Volume (ICVFX)
A structurally-ready stage fitted with an LED wall for real-time 
in-camera visual effects, camera tracking, and traditional ICVFX 
workflows.
STAGE B
AI Grey-Box Stage
A minimal-build stage where actors and essential practical set 
pieces are shot against grey/green, with the surrounding 
environment generated and extended using AI — built from 3D 
Gaussian Splatting captures of the real set, extended 
generatively for full spatial consistency.
The pitch: Productions can use either stage independently, but the real differentiator is using both together — blocking and previz on the grey-box stage, hero LED work on Stage A, 
unified through a shared generative/splatting pipeline.

Stage Breakdown
STAGE A
LED Volume
Currently structurally capable of holding an LED wall; no 
wall installed yet
Target spec: ~100 reconfigurable LED panels — flexible 
wall geometry rather than a fixed single configuration
Vendor candidates: Sony or ROE
Positioned as the dedicated ICVFX stage: real-time 
rendering, camera tracking, in-camera composites
Serves productions that want traditional, proven virtual 
production — the "known quantity" offering inside Hive
STAGE B
AI Grey-Box Stage
Minimal practical build: essential props and set pieces 
only, actors filmed against grey/green
Environment generated and extended around the 
practical elements using AI
Technical backbone: 3D Gaussian Splatting to capture 
real geometry and depth from the practical set, 
combined with generative AI environment extension
Positioned as the innovation differentiator — faster 
iteration, lower physical build cost, environments that 
would be impractical or expensive to build or LED-render

Gaussian Splatting — A Visualization Layer, Not a Render Step
Splatting sits alongside the render pipeline rather than inside it — it's used to see the shot, not to produce the deliverable:
Preproduction
Previz the environment ahead of the shoot
On-Set
Live, simulcam-style preview so the director/DP can see a rough version of the 
composited shot in real time while filming
Where Stage A (LED) Fits In
The strategic bet is that the two stages are stronger together than apart:
1
Grey-Box First
Block scenes, test generative environments, iterate on look 
without burning LED wall time
2
LED Volume for Hero Work
Bring final environments, lighting, and camera moves onto 
Stage A for in-camera final pixel where needed
3
Shared Toolchain
The same generative/splatting pipeline feeds both stages, 
so creative and technical decisions made in grey-box carry 
forward into LED work instead of starting over
This positions the offering as a pipeline, not just two rooms — a stronger pitch to Studio than "two more stages for rent."
