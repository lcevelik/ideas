# Short-Term Rental Tax Strategy vs 401k

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Priority:** Medium
**Tags:** #tax #realestate #short-term-rental #401k #investment

## What

Comparison of 401k tax savings vs short-term rental (STR) tax strategy. Source: social media content from a real estate investor.

## The Core Argument

### 401k Limitations (on $500k salary)
- Max contribution: $24,500/year
- Federal tax bill: ~$141,253
- Tax savings: ~$9,000 (6% of tax problem)
- Money locked until 59.5
- Forced withdrawals at 73 (ordinary income, up to 37%)
- No step-up in basis for heirs
- Kids get 10 years to drain it, taxed on top of their own salaries

### Short-Term Rental Strategy
- Buy property (~$995k example)
- Cost segregation study → identify bonus depreciation ($248,352 example)
- Year-one deductions: $340,676 (bonus depreciation + mortgage interest + operating expenses)
- Qualifies as STR (avg stay < 7 days)
- Material participation > 100 hours/year managing it
- **STR tax loophole**: write off loss against W-2 active income (not just passive)
- Tax savings: $126,050 in year one (14x the 401k savings)
- Annual cash flow: $72,000
- Heirs inherit with stepped-up basis (depreciation wiped clean)

### Key Takeaway
- Take the employer match (free money), but that's where 401k ends
- STRs generate cash flow + tax reduction + asset inheritance
- Repeatable year after year

## Why Investigate

- Tax optimization strategy worth understanding regardless of whether the numbers hold up
- Cost segregation + STR loophole is a real mechanism (verify actual IRS rules)
- Good comparison framework for evaluating real estate vs traditional retirement accounts
- May be relevant for personal tax planning

## Starting Points

- IRS rules on short-term rentals and material participation
- Cost segregation studies — what's actually deductible
- Bonus depreciation phase-out schedule (2026+ rules)
- STR tax loophole: verify the active income offset claim

## Caveats

- This is marketing content promoting a paid webinar — numbers may be cherry-picked or idealized
- $995k property with $248k in bonus depreciation — verify those ratios are realistic
- "Material participation" rules are strict — need to actually manage, not just own
- STR income is not passive for tax purposes but also not truly passive effort
- 2023 TCJA bonus depreciation started phasing out (80% in 2023, 60% in 2024, 40% in 2025, 20% in 2026)
