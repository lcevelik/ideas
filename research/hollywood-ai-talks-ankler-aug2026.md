# Hollywood's Secret AI Talks Go Public — The Ankler (Aug 20, 2026)

*Summary from article by Richard Rushfield — August 20, 2026*

---

## The Story

After 3+ years of secret meetings, a group of Hollywood A-listers and Silicon Valley technologists is going public with a framework called **"Human Generative Workflows" (HGW)**.

The group was organized by **Kathleen Kennedy** (legendary producer) and **Susan Ruskin** (dean of AFI). First meeting was November 2023 at Refik Anadol's LA studio, during the labor strikes. The meetings continued at AFI campus.

Attendees included Oscar-winning filmmakers, celebrated actors, Hollywood C-suite executives, and major names from Silicon Valley. The Ankler's Richard Rushfield and Janice Min attended under off-the-record stipulation.

## The Core Problem

The term "AI" has become so broad that it means everything from a familiar post-production tool to a machine-generated performance replacing an actor. This ambiguity is "showing up in every contract negotiation, credit conversation, guild discussion, and audience disclosure question."

## The Solution: Human Generative Workflows (HGW)

**Central proposition:** Stop treating all AI use as the same thing.

HGW distinguishes between:
- **AI-as-tool:** Generative technology under granular human creative control (evolution of existing digital production)
- **AI-as-replacement:** Purely machine-generated content (what causes industry concern, isn't copyrightable, produces "slop")

**Key quote from a producer:**
> "That's distinct from purely machine-generated content, which is what is causing much of our industry's concern, isn't copyrightable and is where the word 'slop' comes from."

**Another producer:**
> "A lot of us think HGW will be looked back on as an evolution and extension of animation and CGI — requiring the same craft, precision, and skill as any other tools for filmmaking and storytelling."

## What the Document Proposes

Rather than asking "was AI used?" the framework encourages looking at:
- Where human judgment exists
- Where creative direction exists
- Where editorial decision-making exists
- Where accountability exists throughout the filmmaking process

This is an **argument, not yet an industry standard** — described as "an invitation to conversation."

## The 120-Person AFI Event

The weekend before publication, the dialogue expanded to ~120 participants with:
- Open Q&A session for venting apprehensions
- Breakout sessions on authorship, standards, transparency, and control
- Participants included film festival people, Academy people, unions, and production executives

**Susan Ruskin:** "I was so heartened by the honesty... everyone was there for it."

## Context: The Labor Strikes

The meetings started in November 2023, post-strikes. The group was formed to ensure "the future of filmmaking is shaped intentionally, not accidentally" — getting people who regarded each other with suspicion into the same room.

## Key Insight

> "For filmmakers, it's optional whether you use AI, but it's not optional whether or not to understand it."

## What's Next

- This is the group's first public document
- It seeks to "repair the broken conversation on AI"
- Proposed as a framework for better discussion, not a binding standard
- The group intends to continue expanding the dialogue
